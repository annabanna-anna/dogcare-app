# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Professional and side-gig dog sitters/boarders who care for multiple clients' dogs at once. They use the app in short, interrupted bursts throughout the day — between feedings, walks, and meds, often standing in a yard or kitchen with a dog underfoot rather than sitting down to plan. The core job: start a "stay" for a dog, get the right care tasks generated automatically, and check them off (or skip/reschedule) without losing track of which task belongs to which dog when several are boarding at once.

## Product Purpose

Dogcare is a task-tracking companion for dog sitters/boarders. It turns each boarding "stay" into a checklist of scheduled care tasks (feeding, meds, walks, notes) grouped by dog, syncs reminders to the sitter's calendar, and gives an at-a-glance view of who's actively in care and what's left to do today. Success looks like: zero missed feedings/meds across concurrent dogs, and a sitter who can update status in a couple of taps without breaking stride.

## Brand Personality

Bold, playful, poppy — confident and energetic rather than clinical or soft. Established in the July 2026 rebrand (coral `#ff4514` on warm cream, pastel accents, black pill bottom nav, wagging-tail mascot) after the original beige/neutral look read as generic and AI-built. Voice is warm and a little affectionate (dog-forward: paw prints, tail wags) without tipping into cutesy or juvenile — this is a working tool for someone mid-shift, not a kids' app.

## Anti-references

- The original beige/neutral/cream-on-cream palette this app shipped with first — rejected for reading as safe, generic, "AI-built."
- Clinical/dashboard-cold pet-tech (sterile blues, grays, corporate SaaS chrome) — wrong register for a warm, physical, hands-on job.
- Overly cute/juvenile pet-app kitsch (cartoon mascots dominating the UI, baby-talk copy) — the mascot and motion are accents, not the whole identity.

## Design Principles

- **Optimized for interruption, not for planning.** Every core screen (Today, Task Card, Dog Profile) should be scannable and actionable in a glance-and-tap, not a sit-down session.
- **Group by dog, not by task type.** The sitter's mental model is "what does Barkley need," not "what feedings are due" — task grouping and card design should follow the dog.
- **One committed accent color, used deliberately.** Coral carries brand energy and status/emphasis (active, done, progress); pastels (peach/lavender/lemon/mint) differentiate dogs/categories, not compete for attention.
- **Motion earns its keep.** The wagging-tail and chase-spin motifs are the brand's signature gesture — reserve real, purposeful motion for these moments rather than generic fade-ins everywhere.
- **Practical warmth over cuteness.** Playful shapes (pills, rounded cards) and a dog-forward voice, but copy and layout still respect that this is a tool someone relies on to not miss a medication dose.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large/bold text ≥3:1, visible focus states, tap targets sized for one-handed use (sitter's other hand is often occupied with a leash or bowl). Full `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for the tail-wag, chase-spin, and pop-in animations.
